# Form Source Reviewer

Collect reliable evidence from the material attached to or linked from a form before choosing answers. Use the host's available browser or computer-control capability when the source is in the live browser. Pair this skill with `careful-form-responder` when answers will be entered into the form. If the host cannot access or play the required source, report that limitation and do not claim it was reviewed.

Consensus or an equivalent research connector is optional supporting research. Use it only when it is actually available, the user requests or permits outside scientific research, and the question calls for published evidence. Never use outside research as a substitute for a required video, reading, document, or other assigned source.

## Evidence rules

- Review the actual source. A title, thumbnail, filename, search snippet, or surrounding description is not a substitute for its content.
- Distinguish what the source states or shows from inference and general knowledge.
- Never claim to have watched a video, heard audio, read a document, or inspected a visual that was not accessible and actually reviewed.
- Use captions, transcripts, slide text, alt text, or page text as supporting evidence, but verify relevant visual or audio details when the question depends on them.
- If the source is inaccessible, corrupted, paywalled, muted without captions, or blocked by sign-in, stop and tell the user exactly what must be made accessible. Do not guess.
- Keep notes tied to question wording and, when useful, timestamps, page numbers, slide numbers, headings, speakers, or chart labels.

## Connect source questions to the material

1. Read the form question and every answer option before reviewing the source. Identify the specific fact, event, sequence, quote, visual detail, calculation, or interpretation needed.
2. Note whether the question asks about the whole source or a particular segment. Whole-source questions require coverage broad enough to rule out late or contextual evidence.
3. Open the source through the ordinary visible interface. Preserve the form tab and its progress.
4. Review the content in a stable order and record only evidence that can affect an answer.
5. Revisit the exact relevant segment or page before finalizing a low-confidence or easily confused answer.
6. Return a compact evidence note to the form workflow: proposed answer, supporting evidence, location, confidence, and any unresolved ambiguity.

## Video workflow

When a form includes a video and its questions depend on the video, actually play and review it.

1. Inspect the title, duration, player state, available audio, captions, transcript, chapter markers, and playback errors.
2. Start from the beginning unless the question or source clearly identifies a specific segment. Do not jump around randomly.
3. For questions about the video's overall message, sequence, examples, or conclusions, review the full relevant video. Do not answer from the opening minutes alone.
4. Observe both audio and visuals. Slides, demonstrations, gestures, labels, diagrams, and on-screen text may contain evidence not present in captions.
5. Use captions or a transcript to confirm wording and names, while checking the actual playback when tone, speaker identity, timing, action, or imagery matters.
6. Pause when necessary to record a timestamped note. Replay confusing or decisive segments.
7. Confirm playback reached the required end or segment boundary and that no buffering gap hid content.
8. If playback cannot be reviewed reliably, stop and request the user's help rather than inferring from metadata.

Do not increase speed merely to finish quickly when it would impair comprehension. A modest speed adjustment is acceptable only when the material remains fully intelligible and the user has not requested normal-speed viewing.

## Audio workflow

- Confirm the correct clip is playing and audio is available.
- Listen for speaker changes, tone, wording, background sounds, pauses, and sequence when the question depends on them.
- Use a transcript as a cross-check, not as proof of nonverbal audio details.
- Replay the decisive portion and note its timestamp before answering.

## Documents, readings, and slides

- Read headings and instructions first, then the exact relevant section in context.
- Check page or slide numbers, footnotes, captions, tables, and definitions.
- For “according to the reading” questions, prefer the source's wording and scope over outside knowledge.
- When a document contains a claim and a later exception or qualification, preserve the qualification in the answer.
- For scanned or image-based material, visually verify OCR-sensitive items such as numbers, symbols, names, and negations.

## Images, diagrams, and charts

- Inspect the full visual, not only nearby text.
- Identify title, legend, axes, units, scale, categories, labels, annotations, and time period.
- Distinguish direct observations from interpretations.
- For charts, verify the direction, magnitude, baseline, and whether values are exact or approximate.
- For diagrams or sequences, follow arrows and labels in the intended direction and check for repeated or similarly named elements.

## Resolve answer choices carefully

- Compare each candidate answer against the collected evidence, not just against memory.
- Reject distractors using specific contradictions where possible.
- Watch for options that are partly true but too broad, too narrow, reversed, attributed to the wrong speaker, or taken from the wrong time or section.
- When no option exactly matches, recheck the source and question before treating the closest option as intended.
- When the source supports multiple interpretations, state the ambiguity and confidence rather than inventing certainty.

## Evidence note format

Use this compact structure for each source-dependent question:

| Question | Proposed answer | Source location | Supporting evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Keep quotations short and only when exact wording matters. Prefer faithful paraphrase. Make clear whether the answer is directly stated, visually observed, calculated from the source, or inferred.
